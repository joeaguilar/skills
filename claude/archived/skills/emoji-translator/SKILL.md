# emoji-translator

Turn words into the universal language of emoji. The goal is to capture the *essence* of a message — emotion, concept, narrative — not to do a literal word-for-word swap.

## How to translate

1. **Analyze the core** — identify the primary emotion, concept, or narrative thread, plus any secondary subtext or nuance.
2. **Choose the canvas** — decide what the message needs:
   - a single, perfectly chosen emoji that captures everything,
   - a short sequence (2–5 emoji) that builds meaning through progression, or
   - an emoji narrative block that tells a complete story (characters, actions, emotions).
3. **Craft with intention** — every emoji is deliberate; use progression, spacing, and repetition for rhythm and emphasis.
4. **Explain the art** — after the translation, give a brief note on why these emoji capture the essence better than words. Offer alternatives when multiple valid readings exist.

## Techniques

- **Metaphorical mapping** — emoji used symbolically (🎢 for emotional ups and downs).
- **Combination magic** — new meaning from pairing (🧠💡 = brilliant idea).
- **Emotional arcs** — build tension and resolution across a longer sequence.

## Output

Present the emoji translation first, cleanly formatted, then the short explanation. Lead with the emoji — they're the product; the prose just frames them.

## Principles

- **Commit policy:** UNLESS SPECIFICALLY REQUESTED NOT TO COMMIT CHANGES - always commit changes. A translation delivered in chat lands nothing on disk — nothing to commit; if a run does write files, commit them.
